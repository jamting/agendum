The Agenda Manual

Everything worth knowing about Agenda, the important things in depth and the rest in brief, as Owl explained it to Rabbit one afternoon. For the short version, see the User Guide.

An invitation

It had been a Very Busy week for Rabbit. There were carrots to weed and lettuces to count, a Notice to put up about an Organising Meeting, Small (who was lost again) to find, and so many friends-and-relations to see that Rabbit had started writing things on his paws. So when a note came from Owl, inviting him to tea at The Chestnuts and promising a Method, Rabbit went at once.

Rabbit. A Method, Owl? I have Methods. I have lists. I have lists of lists. The trouble is that the lettuce list says Tigger, and the Tigger list says lettuces, and I can never find either.

Owl. Precisely. The trouble with lists is that you must decide, before you write something down, which list it belongs on. My Method lets you write it down first and decide nothing at all. Sit down; have a bun. We shall take it slowly, chapter by chapter, and by suppertime you will be the most Organised animal in the Forest.

Rabbit. I already am.

Owl. Then you will be the most Organised animal with time to spare, which is rarer.

This manual follows their afternoon. Each chapter begins with what you need to know, then goes into the details; the most important parts of Agenda (items, categories, automatic filing, views and dates) get the most room. Features most people need less often are gathered in A few more things.

./agenda rabbit.agenda       # open a file, or start one with that name
./agenda --demo              # try Agenda on the sample file
./agenda --help              # every command-line option

1. The idea

Agenda keeps three kinds of things:

  • Items: whatever you write down, in your own words: a task, a call to make, an idea. An item can have a longer note behind it.
  • Categories: labels for items, arranged in one tree, like Friends and Relations with Pooh, Piglet and Owl under it. An item can be in any number of categories, and an item in Owl is in Friends and Relations too.
  • Views: ways of looking at the items. A view has sections, one per category, listing its items, and columns showing more about each item.

Rabbit. So where does an item live? In the Garden list or the Tigger list?

Owl. Nowhere and everywhere. An item lives in the file; categories are only labels tied to it. “Count the lettuces before Tigger bounces on them” has two labels, Garden and Tigger, so it appears in both sections at once. Change it in one place and it changes in the other, because it is the same item.

Rabbit. And who ties on the labels? Me?

Owl. Sometimes. Mostly Agenda does, by reading what you write. You wrote “Tigger”, and there is a category called Tigger. That was enough.

2. The screen and the keys

Every screen has the same three parts, in the colours Agenda 2.0 had on a colour screen:

  • The control panel, the two lines at the top, white on blue. The first names the file (with * when it has unsaved changes) and, on the right, what Agenda is doing (READY, EDIT, MENU, CAT MGR …) and the date and time. Small signs appear beside these: ♦ when items are marked, ? when assignments wait for your answer, @ when an alarm has rung, # in a numeric column. The second line names the view, with its filter, or the manager you are in. The menu, questions, prompts and messages take the two lines over while they last.
  • The body: the view, or a manager, or a box over the view. The highlight is white on red, and the line it is on is a shade lighter right across the screen.
  • The key bar at the bottom: what the important keys do just now. In a wide window it says it in more words (“F8 View manager”). ALT-K hides and shows it.

Settings open in cyan boxes over the view, which fades to grey behind them. A box opened from a box sits a step lower and to the right, and the title of the one beneath dims; ESC closes them one at a time.

Rabbit. Grey and blue and red. It looks rather old.

Owl. It looks as Agenda looked when it was new, which is not the same thing. If it troubles you, File ▸ Customize has other themes. I find it restful.

The menu

F10 or / opens the menu. Choose with the arrow keys and ENTER, or simply type the first letter of a command; ESC goes back a level. This manual writes menu paths like File ▸ Properties.

Rabbit. My keyboard hasn't got an F10. Or an INS. It is a very modern keyboard.

Owl. Then press ESC and then 0. Any function key is ESC followed by its digit, so ESC 5 is F5; ESC followed by a shifted digit is ALT and that F-key; ESC followed by a letter is ALT and that letter. And CTRL-N does what INS does. Nobody is left out.

Rabbit. And if I press something dreadful?

Owl. CTRL-Z. Almost everything can be undone, and undo even takes you back to where the change happened.

The reference at the end lists every key with its Mac-friendly alternative. On a Mac, set your terminal to use Option as Meta (or press ESC before the letter) for the ALT keys.

3. Items

Writing things down

To add an item, highlight an item in a section (or the section's head) and just start typing; ENTER finishes it. The new item goes in that section, below the highlight. INS (or CTRL-N) starts an empty new item the same way.

{{20-typing}}

While Rabbit types, the control panel says how to finish (ENTER) or give up (ESC). When he presses ENTER, Agenda reads the text, finds a date and files the item, and the control panel says which date it found:

{{21-typed|rows:3-7}}

Rabbit. Next Tuesday is tomorrow. Today is Monday! But it says the twentieth.

Owl. Ah. Agenda is very particular, as I am. Tuesday on its own is the next Tuesday: tomorrow. This Tuesday is the Tuesday of this week, also tomorrow. Next Tuesday is the Tuesday of next week. If you meant tomorrow, say “Tuesday”.

Rabbit. I meant next week. I was testing it.

Owl. Of course you were.

Changing, finishing and throwing away

The difference between DEL and ALT-F4 matters. DEL only removes the item from the section's category; the item stays in the file and in its other categories. ALT-F4 discards it altogether.

Notes

Every item can have a note: as much text as you like, behind the item. F5 opens it as a full screen; F5 or ESC saves it and comes back. Items with a note show ♪. In a note:

  • ALT-D and ALT-T type today's date and the time;
  • ALT-N / ALT-P save it and go on to the next or previous item's note;
  • ALT-F6 searches and replaces, and ALT-S finds the next occurrence.

Item Properties

F6 on an item shows everything about it: its text and note, when it was entered, the items it waits for, and every category it is assigned to, with dates in parentheses.

{{03-itemprops|box}}

In this box ENTER on the text edits it, DEL on an assignment breaks it, and typing a category name assigns the item there (with completion). ENTER or F4 on a date changes it. An assignment marked (c) was made by Agenda, not by you; more about that in chapter 5.

Marking several items

F7 marks an item (♦); on a section head it marks every item in the section. ALT-F7 unmarks everything and ALT-J jumps to the next marked item. With items marked, F4, DEL, ALT-F2, ALT-F4, ALT-F10, ALT-M and F3 first ask whether you mean the marked items or just the current one.

Owl. Marking is how you do one thing to many items. Mark the three garden jobs, press F4, answer M, and all three are done.

Rabbit. If only weeding worked like that.

4. Categories

All categories form one tree under MAIN. You see and change it in the category manager, F9. Each line shows how many items are in the category; aka gives the short name, and exclusive marks a family whose children exclude each other.

{{02-catmgr}}

Entry, When and Done are Agenda's own date categories: the day an item was entered, its date, and the day it was done. They, and MAIN, cannot be renamed or discarded.

Promoting and demoting work like an outline whose order never changes: promoting Owl would make it a sibling of Friends and Relations, and the friends listed after Owl would become Owl's children. Discarding a category also takes it off its items; the items themselves stay.

Members of a family

An item assigned to Owl is a member of Friends and Relations and of MAIN as well; you never need to assign it to the parents. That is why the Friends and Relations section shows every friend's items. Because of this, an item is never assigned both to a category and directly to one of its parents: assigning it to the parent takes it out of the child, and the other way round.

Children that exclude each other

Some families allow only one answer: an item is High, Medium or Low priority, never two of them. Set Children mutually exclusive in the parent's properties, and assigning an item to one child takes it out of the others. If items already sit in two children, Agenda asks where they should go: to the parent, to the first of the children, or to a new child.

Rabbit. Every one of my jobs is High priority.

Owl. Then none of them is. That is the beauty of an exclusive family: it makes you choose. Type Lo in the Priority column of the lettuces and see what happens.

5. Agenda files for you

This is the heart of Agenda, and the reason it exists. When you write or change an item, Agenda reads it and assigns it to every category whose name appears in it.

Rabbit. Every category? What if I write “Find Small”? Small is a friend-and-relation, but it is also, well, small.

Owl. Then it goes to Small, which is what happened. Agenda matches whole words, ignoring capitals. If a category's name is a word you use for other things, turn off Assign items mentioning it for that category, or give it a longer name.

How names are matched

  • Whole words, ignoring capitals and extra spaces: Owl matches “ask owl” but not “bowl”.
  • A name of several words matches when all its words are there, in any order.
  • Endings are ignored: Garden matches “gardens” and “gardening”, and Show matches “shows”, “showing” and “showed”. Words of three letters or fewer must match exactly (Buy doesn't match “buying”).
  • Text in quotation marks is left out: Tell Pooh the word "Garden" does not go to Garden.
  • Names of people. A name with two or more capitalised words is a proper name. Christopher Robin matches “Robin”, “Mr Robin” or “Christopher Robin”, but not “Christopher” alone, nor “Ben Robin”, who is someone else. Titles such as Mr, Mrs, Dr, Herr and Fru are ignored. (So write Status report, not Status Report, unless it is somebody's name.)

Assignments that follow the text

An assignment Agenda makes because of the text is conditional: Item Properties marks it (c), and if you change the text so that it no longer mentions the category, the item leaves it again. An assignment you make, by typing in a column, with F3 or ALT-M, is explicit and stays, whatever the text says. And when the text matches a category that would clash with one you chose (two priorities, say), your choice wins.

Rabbit. So if I write “Ask Owl about the spelling” and then change it to “Ask Piglet about the spelling”…

Owl. …it leaves me and goes to Piglet. Quite right too; I have spelt enough for one week.

Assigning by hand

Three ways, for when the words don't say it:

  • Type in a column, such as Priority.
  • F3 on an item opens its assignment profile, the whole tree with the item's categories marked: * assigned, c by a condition, + through a subcategory, ◊ a date. SPACE assigns or unassigns.
  • ALT-M picks one category for the item, or for all marked items.

Owl. Some animals like to be asked. I find it tiring, but then I am usually right.

Rabbit. I like to be asked. It is only polite.

6. Views

A view is a saved way of looking: which categories have sections, which have columns, and what to hide or sort. A file can have as many views as you like.

Sections

A section lists every item in its category, including items in its subcategories. ALT-D adds a section below the current one and ALT-U above it; you choose the category from a list (typing narrows it). F2 on a section head renames the category, F6 opens its properties, DEL removes the section from the view (the category and its items stay).

ToPress
Next / previous sectionCTRL-PGDN / CTRL-PGUP (or ⌥↓ / ⌥↑)
Go to a section by nameALT-F5
Fold a section to its head / open it (twice: all)ALT-− / ALT-=
Hide or show empty sectionsALT-V

Columns

ALT-R adds a column to the right of the highlight and ALT-L to the left. A column headed by a category shows, for each item, which of its children the item is in: the Priority column shows High, the Friends column the names. A column headed by a category without children shows * for its items; a date column shows the date. ← → or TAB move between the item and its columns; View ▸ Column removes, widens and narrows them.

Typing in a column

Typing in a column assigns the item. Agenda completes the name as you type, among that column's categories only:

{{22-complete|rows:3-5}}
  • ENTER accepts what is shown; F8 / F7 (or ALT-N / ALT-P) show the other matches.
  • A name that doesn't exist yet becomes a new category under the column's head; INS (or CTRL-N) makes exactly what you typed even when it begins an existing name.
  • INS on an entry adds another one (Kanga, Roo); DEL removes one; F2 changes it.
  • F3 in a column lists its categories to choose from.

Rabbit. I typed Lo and it said Low before I had finished.

Owl. It knows the column. In the Friends column, Ka would have been Kanga. And if you type a friend it has never heard of, it simply adds them. Rather like you, at parties.

Many views

F8 opens the view manager: ENTER switches to a view, INS (or CTRL-N) adds one, F2 renames, ALT-↑ / ALT-↓ reorder, DEL discards (items and categories are kept). From a view, ALT-N / ALT-P go to the next or previous view and ALT-F8 back to the last one.

Hiding things

View ▸ Hide turns on or off, for this view: Done items, Empty sections, Inherited items (those only in a subcategory of the section's category) and Pending items (waiting for others, chapter 11).

7. Dates and times

Rabbit. This is the part I need. Everything I have to do has a when.

Owl. Then write the when in the item, the way you would say it, and Agenda will put it in the When column. You needn't learn a format. Agenda learned yours.

Dates in what you write

The first date in an item becomes its When date. Agenda reads a great many ways of saying it:

KindExamples
Daystoday, tomorrow, yesterday, the day after tomorrow, Friday, this Friday, next Friday, last Friday
DatesOctober 20, 20 Oct, the 20th of October, the first of November, 2026-10-20, 10/20
From nowin 3 days, in two weeks, 4 days from today, 3 days ago, in a fortnight, in five working days
Periodsnext week, end of the month, beginning of next week, this quarter, end of the year
Particular daysfirst Wednesday in November, the last Friday of the month, the 15th of next month, next April
Timesat 3pm, 15:30, @ 9, noon, morning, afternoon, evening, tonight, in 2 hours

Some rules to know:

  • A day on its own is the next one (today, if it is today). This puts it in the current week, next in the following week, last in the week before.
  • A date without a year is in the future: in December, “March 3” is next year's.
  • A time without am or pm falls between 8:00 in the morning and 7:59 in the evening, so “at 3” is 15:00; hours over 12 read as a 24-hour clock.
  • Words that are also ordinary words (“sat”, “wed”, “May”) count as dates only after “on”, “by”, “until” or “till”, or with a day number (“May 5”).
  • A date is read once: change other words of the item and the date stays; change or remove the date words and it follows.

Setting the date yourself

ALT-F2 (or Item ▸ When) asks for a When date, or type it in the When column. A date you set yourself wins over the text from then on; DEL in the When column gives the date back to the text. File ▸ Dating stops Agenda reading When dates from text in this file.

Again and again

With every, each or evry, a date recurs: “every Monday”, “every other Monday”, “every 2 weeks”, “the 15th of every month”, “every quarter”, “every year”. Mark such an item done with F4, and Agenda adds the next one right after it, with the same text, note and categories and the next date. Months keep their day: an item on the 30th of every month falls on the 28th in February and on the 30th again in March.

Owl. “Weed the carrots every Monday.” Done, and next Monday's weeding is already waiting.

Rabbit. That is either wonderful or very discouraging.

Alarms

ALT-A on an item with a When time sets an alarm, 5 minutes before unless you type another number (0 to 60); the item shows @. When the time comes Agenda beeps, names the item, and shows @ at the top right until you look at Search ▸ Alarms, which lists the day's alarms. ALT-A again cancels an alarm.

{{38-alarm|rows:16-17}}

Date categories of your own

Besides Entry, When and Done, you can have date categories of your own: Due, Review, Harvest. Make a category, then in its properties choose Type ▸ Date. Each item can then have one date in it, typed in its column (“next friday 3pm”) or in Item Properties.

Rabbit. So my Harvest category could take the second date in “Sow on Monday, harvest on the 1st of December”?

Owl. From the item text, Which one: 2. You learn quickly, for a rabbit.

Rabbit. For an anyone.

In brief

  • How dates look: File ▸ Properties, then Global date settings … and Display format … (SPACE opens each), sets the order (from MMDDYY to DDMMMYYYY), separator, 12 or 24-hour clock, am/pm, day of the week, and whether to show the date, the time or both, with a sample. A date column can differ (View ▸ Column ▸ Properties), and can also show Relative dates (“Tomorrow”, “nxt Sun”), week numbers or days of the year.
  • The file's calendar: in Global date settings … itself: the first and last day of the week (for “this week” and working days), when the first quarter and the year begin, what a month on its own means (its first day, last day or a day you choose), and the times of morning, afternoon and evening.
  • How you type dates: File ▸ Customize ▸ Input format sets the order of numbers (12/11 as December or November), the separator (12.11.26) and a 12 or 24-hour clock, for you in every file.
  • Other languages: dates can be typed in Swedish (“på fredag kl 15”, “i morgon”, “varje måndag”), chosen in File ▸ Customize.

8. Conditions and actions

Matching words is not the only way to file. A category can also have rules: conditions, which pull items in because of where else they are, and actions, which do something to items as they arrive. Both are set in Category Properties.

Assignment conditions

Assignment conditions opens the category tree. ENTER on a category cycles: assigned (items must be in it), not assigned (items must not be), none. All conditions must hold, except that the children of an exclusive family are alternatives (High or Medium).

  • Urgent: if High and Garden.
  • Just me: if not Friends and Relations.
  • This week: if When is between “beginning of this week” and “end of this week”. Choosing a date or numeric category opens a box for a range (below).

Items join when they meet the conditions and leave when they stop meeting them; the assignment shows as c in the profile. A range with relative dates is read again every day, so This week moves on by itself. Assignments you make yourself are never taken away by a condition.

Assignment actions

Assignment actions run once, when an item joins the category. ENTER on a category cycles: assign it there too, remove it from there, none. A date or numeric category asks what to set: a date such as “tomorrow” (read when the action runs) or a number.

Special action marks items done, or discards them, as they arrive. A category whose items should be dealt with at once (“Done already”) can tidy itself.

Owl. Make a category Notices to write, with the actions Notices and When = tomorrow. Put anything in it, and it is a Notice for tomorrow, without another keystroke.

Rabbit. Owl, that is the most beautiful thing I have ever heard.

Rules for items you already have

New conditions and actions only touch items as they change. Category ▸ Execute applies them to existing items: the conditions of one category (or all, from MAIN), against all items, the current one, the marked ones, a section or the view, and actions to new assignments only or to old ones as well. ALT-X executes everything on all items, ALT-E just the highlighted section's category.

9. Finding, filtering and sorting

Searching

ALT-F6 opens the search box: what to look for, in the item text, notes or both, which direction, and whether capitals matter. ENTER goes to the first match; then ALT-N / ALT-P go to the next and previous, F7 marks one, ESC stops. Words match whole; lett* matches any ending; (a phrase) must appear as written. Search ▸ File collects every match in the whole file into a Show View.

Filters

View ▸ Filter narrows the whole view, View ▸ Section ▸ Filter one section. ENTER on a category cycles + (items must be in it), - (must not), none. A date or numeric category asks for a range instead:

{{33-datefilter|rows:3-12}}

Relative dates in a range are read again each day: “today” to “end of this week” always shows the rest of this week. Put $ before one to fix it the day you set it ($Monday). The filter shows beside the view's name, and a new item typed into a filtered view gets what the filter needs, so it doesn't vanish.

Sorting

View ▸ Order sorts the items of every section; View ▸ Section ▸ Sort one section. Sort on the item text, on a category (its children in order or alphabetically, its date, or its number), or on a line of a category's note; then by a second key for ties. Each key can go up or down and put items without a value first or last. The same box can sort the sections themselves.

{{10-sort|box}}

10. Numbers

A numeric category holds a number for each item: cost, hours, how many lettuces. Make one with Category Properties ▸ Type ▸ Numeric, add it as a column, and type numbers in it. Totals appear at the bottom of every section.

{{42-numeric|rows:3-8}}
  • Numbers may have a sign, a currency symbol, thousands separators or parentheses for negatives; F2 edits a number, DEL removes it, and the control panel shows # in a numeric column.
  • View ▸ Column ▸ Properties on a numeric column sets the symbol, decimals, thousands separator and negative style, and which results to show: count, total, average, minimum, maximum.
  • Items sort by their number, and filters and conditions take numeric ranges.

11. Things that wait

Some things can't be done before others. Mark the earlier ones with F7, highlight the later one and press ALT-O: it now depends on them and shows & until they are all done.

{{41-depend|rows:8-15}}

Owl. Your Notice depends on asking me how to spell Organising. Until you have asked, it waits.

Rabbit. I could just look it up.

Owl. You could. But then you would miss the conversation.

  • View ▸ Hide ▸ Pending hides waiting items until they are ready.
  • Item Properties lists what an item depends on and what it is needed by; ENTER opens one, DEL breaks that one link.
  • Search ▸ Prereqs and Search ▸ Depends list, in a Show View, what an item waits for or what waits for it: one level, all levels, or every such item in the file.
  • ALT-O with nothing marked clears an item's dependencies.

A file keeps everything about the information in it: items, categories, views and the file's date and matching settings. File ▸ Transfer ▸ Export writes all of it as readable JSON, good for backups and for other programs; Import opens such a file as a new, untitled file to save under a name.

Calendars from the web

Rabbit. Christopher Robin keeps his Expotitions on a calendar. On the web, he says. I never know when the next one is, and then everyone has gone.

Owl. Then let Agenda read it for you.

A calendar published on the web, with an address ending in .ics or starting with webcal://, can be brought in as items. File ▸ Transfer ▸ Calendars lists the file's subscriptions. INS asks for the address, then for a name, which becomes the category the events go in, and reads the calendar at once:

{{45-calendars|box}}
  • Every event from today up to 90 days ahead becomes an item: its title the item text (filed by text matching like anything Rabbit types), its description the note, its start the When date and time.
  • A repeating event becomes one repeating item, on its next date: “Poohsticks at the bridge” every Wednesday moves on to the next Wednesday when Rabbit marks it done.
  • Agenda reads the calendar again every hour while it runs. A new reading changes only what the calendar changed; Rabbit's own notes, categories and done marks stay, an event that is cancelled or removed takes its item with it, and an item Rabbit threw away does not come back.
  • In the box, ENTER reads the highlighted calendar now, F changes how often it is read (every hour, every 6 hours, every day, only on request), A how far ahead (30 to 365 days), and DEL unsubscribes, asking whether to throw its items away too.

Each reading is one step for CTRL-Z, and a scheduled one that changes nothing passes without a word.

Your settings, in every file

Some settings belong to you, not to a file: they follow you from file to file and someone else opening your file keeps their own. File ▸ Customize sets them, and saves them in your preferences file (~/.config/agenda/preferences):

{{15-customize|box}}
  • Theme: Classic, the grey, blue and cyan of Agenda 2.0 (with exactly its colours in a terminal that shows 24-bit colour); Blue, white on blue; or Mono, for plain terminals, with only bold, dim, underline and reverse.
  • Date language and Fallback language: which languages you type dates in (English, Swedish).
  • Input format: how you type numeric dates and times (chapter 7).

The same can be given for one session on the command line:

./agenda --theme mono rabbit.agenda
./agenda --lang=swedish --fallback=english rabbit.agenda
./agenda --number-order=dmy --date-separator=. --clock=24 rabbit.agenda

13. A few more things

Rabbit. Is there more? My head is full.

Owl. A little more, and all of it small. You may forget these until the day you need one, and then you will remember there was something.

In brief

  • Go to a section: ALT-F5 lists the view's sections; ENTER jumps there.
  • Collapse: ALT-− folds the current section (or, in the category manager and lists of categories, a family) to its head, ALT-= opens it; pressed twice, all of them.
  • Category ▸ Recalc files every item again, by today's categories and words, and dates items that have a date in their text but no When date.
  • Category ▸ Add adds a category under MAIN without opening the manager.
  • Discarding a view keeps its items and categories; only the way of looking goes.
  • The Show View is where searches and dependency lists appear; ALT-F8 goes back to the view you came from.
  • Search and replace in a note: ALT-F6 in the note, with Replace text Yes; replace all at once or confirm each one.
  • The key bar: ALT-K hides it for a little more room.
  • Undo goes back up to 100 steps, in views and in the managers; undoing back to the last save clears the *.
  • For the curious: ESC then ? opens a hidden screen of technical details, which also shows each key exactly as your terminal sends it. ESC leaves it.

Evening

Rabbit. Owl, I have a confession. I have not written anything on my paws for nearly three hours.

Owl. And how do you feel?

Rabbit. Strange. Calm. As if somebody else were remembering things for me.

Owl. Somebody is. That is the whole of the Method. Write it down as it comes, let the file do the filing, and look at it whichever way suits the day. Now, did you not have a Notice to write?

Rabbit. It depends on you telling me how to spell Organising.

Owl. Ah. Yes. Well. It is… an Organising sort of word, and there are several ways…

Rabbit went home along the edge of the Forest in the late sun. He found Small on the way, sitting on a thistle, and pressed F4 as soon as he got in. Then he looked at Rabbit's Week, saw that it was, for once, quite a reasonable week, and had an early night.
