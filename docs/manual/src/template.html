Wol's Agendum: the Manual

Everything worth knowing about Wol's Agendum, the important things in depth and the rest in brief, as Owl explained it to Rabbit one afternoon. For the short version, see the User Guide.

An invitation

In Which Rabbit Is Invited to Tea at The Chestnuts and Promised a Method

It had been a Very Busy week for Rabbit. There were carrots to weed and lettuces to count, a Notice to put up about an Organising Meeting, Small (who was lost again) to find, and so many friends-and-relations to see that Rabbit had started writing things on his paws. So when a note came from Owl, inviting him to tea at The Chestnuts and promising a Method, Rabbit went at once.

Rabbit. A Method, Owl? I have Methods. I have lists. I have lists of lists. The trouble is that the lettuce list says Tigger, and the Tigger list says lettuces, and I can never find either.

Owl. Precisely. The trouble with lists is that you must decide, before you write something down, which list it belongs on. My Method lets you write it down first and decide nothing at all. Sit down; have a bun. We shall take it slowly, chapter by chapter, and by suppertime you will be the most Organised animal in the Forest.

Rabbit. I already am.

Owl. Then you will be the most Organised animal with time to spare, which is rarer.

Rabbit. Has it a name, this Method?

Owl. It is called Wol's Agendum. W-O-L, which is how I spell it. An agendum is one thing to be done, and you have a great many. Call it Agendum, if you are in a hurry, which you always are. It is modelled on a famous old program called Agenda, and when I say “as Agenda did”, that is the one I mean.

This manual follows their afternoon. Each chapter begins with what you need to know, then goes into the details; the most important parts of Agendum (items, categories, automatic filing, views and dates) get the most room. Features most people need less often are gathered in A few more things.

./agendum rabbit.agendum     # open a file, or start one with that name
./agendum                    # ask which file, offering the last one used
./agendum --rabbit           # open Rabbit's file, the one in this manual
./agendum --demo             # or the sample from Agenda's patent
./agendum --help             # every command-line option

Agendum begins with a title screen, as Agenda did, which goes after a moment or at any key. Started without a file, it asks File to open or create and offers the file used last, so most days ENTER is all it takes. A name that doesn't exist yet starts a new file there.

Rabbit. “Hoo's in control? You are.” Is that a spelling mistake, Owl?

Owl. It is a motto. Mottoes are allowed to be spelt distinctively.

1. The idea

In Which an Item Lives Nowhere and Everywhere, and Tigger Is Enough

Agendum keeps three kinds of things:

  • Items: whatever you write down, in your own words: a task, a call to make, an idea. An item can have a longer note behind it.
  • Categories: labels for items, arranged in one tree, like Friends and Relations with Pooh, Piglet and Owl under it. An item can be in any number of categories, and an item in Owl is in Friends and Relations too.
  • Views: ways of looking at the items. A view has sections, one per category, listing its items, and columns showing more about each item.

Rabbit. So where does an item live? In the Garden list or the Tigger list?

Owl. Nowhere and everywhere. An item lives in the file; categories are only labels tied to it. “Count the lettuces before Tigger bounces on them” has two labels, Garden and Tigger, so it appears in both sections at once. Change it in one place and it changes in the other, because it is the same item.

Rabbit. And who ties on the labels? Me?

Owl. Sometimes. Mostly Agendum does, by reading what you write. You wrote “Tigger”, and there is a category called Tigger. That was enough.

2. The screen and the keys

In Which Rabbit's Very Modern Keyboard Has No F10, and Nobody Is Left Out

Every screen has the same three parts, in the colours Agenda 2.0 had on a colour screen:

  • The control panel, the two lines at the top, white on blue. The first names the file (with * when it has unsaved changes) and, on the right, what Agendum is doing (READY, EDIT, MENU, CAT MGR …) and the date and time. Small signs appear beside these: ♦ when items are marked, ? when assignments wait for your answer, @ when an alarm has rung, # in a numeric column. The second line names the view, with its filter, or the manager you are in. The menu, questions, prompts and messages take the two lines over while they last.
  • The body: the view, or a manager, or a box over the view. The highlight is white on red, and the line it is on is a shade lighter right across the screen.
  • The key bar at the bottom: what the important keys do just now. In a wide window it says it in more words (“F8 View manager”). ALT-K hides and shows it.

Settings open in cyan boxes over the view, which fades to grey behind them. A box opened from a box sits a step lower and to the right, and the title of the one beneath dims; ESC closes them one at a time.

Every settings box works the same way, as Agenda's did:

  • The arrow keys move between its settings, across a row and then on to the next (TAB and SHIFT-TAB too); HOME and END go to the first and the last. While a setting is highlighted, the control panel says what it does and which keys change it.
  • SPACE changes the setting to its next choice, or opens it: a setting showing ... is a box of its own. A letter picks the next choice beginning with it, and F3 lists all the choices. F2 edits a name or a text; a number, such as a width, is simply typed.
  • Changes show at once, so you can see what they do. ENTER keeps them; ESC cancels the box and puts everything back as it was when it opened. The bottom edge of each box says so: “Press ENTER when done, ESC to cancel”.

A question that wants yes or no comes in a small box of its own, the answer highlighted: Y or N answers at once, SPACE switches the answer shown and ENTER accepts it.

Rabbit. And if I change six settings and then wish I hadn't?

Owl. ESC, and all six go back. A box is a place to try things. Only ENTER means it.

Rabbit. Grey and blue and red. It looks rather old.

Owl. It looks as Agenda looked when it was new, which is not the same thing. If it troubles you, File ▸ Customize has other themes. I find it restful.

The menu

F10 or / opens the menu. Choose with the arrow keys and ENTER, or simply type the first letter of a command; ESC goes back a level. A box chosen from the menu goes back to the menu on ESC, so you can back out a step at a time. This manual writes menu paths like File ▸ Properties.

Rabbit. My keyboard hasn't got an F10. Or an INS. It is a very modern keyboard.

Owl. Then press ESC and then 0. Any function key is ESC followed by its digit, so ESC 5 is F5; ESC followed by a shifted digit is ALT and that F-key; ESC followed by a letter is ALT and that letter. And CTRL-N does what INS does. Nobody is left out.

Rabbit. And if I press something dreadful?

Owl. CTRL-Z. Almost everything can be undone, and undo even takes you back to where the change happened.

The reference at the end lists every key with its Mac-friendly alternative. On a Mac, set your terminal to use Option as Meta (or press ESC before the letter) for the ALT keys.

3. Items

In Which Next Tuesday Turns Out to Be the Twentieth, and Rabbit Was Only Testing

Writing things down

To add an item, highlight an item in a section (or the section's head) and just start typing; ENTER finishes it. The new item goes in that section, below the highlight. INS (or CTRL-N) starts an empty new item the same way.

{{20-typing}}

While Rabbit types, the control panel says how to finish (ENTER) or give up (ESC). When he presses ENTER, Agendum reads the text, finds a date and files the item, and the control panel says which date it found:

{{21-typed|rows:3-7}}

Rabbit. Next Tuesday is tomorrow. Today is Monday! But it says the twentieth.

Owl. Ah. Agendum is very particular, as I am. Tuesday on its own is the next Tuesday: tomorrow. This Tuesday is the Tuesday of this week, also tomorrow. Next Tuesday is the Tuesday of next week. If you meant tomorrow, say “Tuesday”.

Rabbit. I meant next week. I was testing it.

Owl. Of course you were.

Rabbit. Copy? But then I shall have two of them, and do one, and forget the other.

Owl. No: ALT-F9 puts the same item in a second section, by assigning it to that section's category too. Mark it done in one place and it is done in both. One item, two places to find it.

Rabbit. And ALT-H takes me from one to the other.

Owl. Hop, as it is called. I thought you would like that.

Notes

Every item can have a note: as much text as you like, behind the item. F5 opens it as a full screen; F5 or ESC saves it and comes back. Items with a note show ♪. In a note:

  • ALT-D and ALT-T type today's date and the time;
  • ALT-N / ALT-P save it and go on to the next or previous item's note;
  • ALT-F6 searches and replaces, and ALT-S finds the next occurrence.

Text in a note is cut and pasted with Agenda's keys. F7 starts marking at the cursor; move the cursor and the marked text stretches with it, over several lines if you like, and F7 again stops. Then F3 copies it, F4 cuts it, ALT-F4 deletes it without keeping it, and F2 pastes what was last copied or cut, at the cursor, in this note or another, as often as you like. With nothing marked, Agendum asks whether you mean the word, the line or all the text.

A note that has grown a list can give it away. ALT-F7 makes the marked text (or the word, the line or all of it) a new item, below the note's item in the same section, and takes it out of the note. ALT-F2 makes a new category of it, under a parent you pick from the tree; the note keeps its text.

Rabbit. My note on “Plan the harvest” has nine things in it now. They ought to be items, I suppose.

Owl. Put the cursor on one, ALT-F7, Line. It leaves the note and stands in your Garden section on its own, ready to be filed and dated. Eight more times, and the note is tidy and so are you.

Item Properties

F6 on an item shows everything about it: its text and note, when it was entered, the items it waits for, and every category it is assigned to, with dates in parentheses.

{{03-itemprops|box}}

In this box F2 on the text edits it, SPACE on the note opens it, DEL on an assignment breaks it, and typing a category name assigns the item there (with completion). F4 on a date or a number changes it. ENTER closes the box. An assignment marked (c) was made by Agendum, not by you; more about that in chapter 5.

Marking several items

F7 marks an item (♦); on a section head it marks every item in the section. ALT-F7 unmarks everything and ALT-J jumps to the next marked item. With items marked, F4, DEL, ALT-F2, ALT-F4, ALT-F9, ALT-F10, ALT-M and F3 first ask whether you mean the marked items or just the current one.

Owl. Marking is how you do one thing to many items. Mark the three garden jobs, press F4, answer M, and all three are done.

Rabbit. If only weeding worked like that.

4. Categories

In Which Every One of Rabbit's Jobs Is High Priority, and So None of Them Is

All categories form one tree under MAIN. You see and change it in the category manager, F9. Each line shows how many items are in the category; aka gives the short name, and exclusive marks a family whose children exclude each other.

{{02-catmgr}}

Entry, When and Done are Agendum's own date categories: the day an item was entered, its date, and the day it was done. They, and MAIN, cannot be renamed or discarded.

Promoting and demoting work like an outline whose order never changes: promoting Owl would make it a sibling of Friends and Relations, and the friends listed after Owl would become Owl's children. Discarding a category also takes it off its items; the items themselves stay.

Members of a family

An item assigned to Owl is a member of Friends and Relations and of MAIN as well; you never need to assign it to the parents. That is why the Friends and Relations section shows every friend's items. Because of this, an item is never assigned both to a category and directly to one of its parents: assigning it to the parent takes it out of the child, and the other way round.

Children that exclude each other

Some families allow only one answer: an item is High, Medium or Low priority, never two of them. Set Exclusive children in the parent's properties, and assigning an item to one child takes it out of the others. If items already sit in two children, Agendum asks where they should go: to the parent, to the first of the children, or to a new child.

Rabbit. Every one of my jobs is High priority.

Owl. Then none of them is. That is the beauty of an exclusive family: it makes you choose. Type Lo in the Priority column of the lettuces and see what happens.

5. Agendum files for you

In Which Small Is Filed Under Small, and Owl Has Spelt Enough for One Week

This is the heart of Agendum, as it was of Agenda, and the reason it exists. When you write or change an item, Agendum reads it and assigns it to every category whose name appears in it.

Rabbit. Every category? What if I write “Find Small”? Small is a friend-and-relation, but it is also, well, small.

Owl. Then it goes to Small, which is what happened. Agendum matches whole words, ignoring capitals. If a category's name is a word you use for other things, turn off Match category name for that category, or give it a longer name.

How names are matched

  • Whole words, ignoring capitals and extra spaces: Owl matches “ask owl” but not “bowl”.
  • A name of several words matches when all its words are there, in any order.
  • Endings are ignored: Garden matches “gardens” and “gardening”, and Show matches “shows”, “showing” and “showed”. Words of three letters or fewer must match exactly (Buy doesn't match “buying”).
  • Text in quotation marks is left out: Tell Pooh the word "Garden" does not go to Garden.
  • Names of people. A name with two or more capitalised words is a proper name. Christopher Robin matches “Robin”, “Mr Robin” or “Christopher Robin”, but not “Christopher” alone, nor “Ben Robin”, who is someone else. Titles such as Mr, Mrs, Dr, Herr and Fru are ignored. (So write Status report, not Status Report, unless it is somebody's name.)

Assignments that follow the text

An assignment Agendum makes because of the text is conditional: Item Properties marks it (c), and if you change the text so that it no longer mentions the category, the item leaves it again. An assignment you make, by typing in a column, with F3 or ALT-M, is explicit and stays, whatever the text says. And when the text matches a category that would clash with one you chose (two priorities, say), your choice wins.

Rabbit. So if I write “Ask Owl about the spelling” and then change it to “Ask Piglet about the spelling”…

Owl. …it leaves me and goes to Piglet. Quite right too; I have spelt enough for one week.

Assigning by hand

Three ways, for when the words don't say it:

  • Type in a column, such as Priority.
  • F3 on an item opens its assignment profile, the whole tree with the item's categories marked: * assigned, c by a condition, + through a subcategory, ◊ a date. SPACE assigns or unassigns.
  • ALT-M picks one category for the item, or for all marked items.

Owl. Some animals like to be asked. I find it tiring, but then I am usually right.

6. Views

In Which Rabbit Types Lo and Gets Low Before He Has Finished

A view is a saved way of looking: which categories have sections, which have columns, and what to hide or sort. A file can have as many views as you like.

Sections

A section lists every item in its category, including items in its subcategories. ALT-D adds a section below the current one and ALT-U above it; you choose the category from a list (typing narrows it). F2 on a section head renames the category, F6 opens the section's properties (below), DEL removes the section from the view (the category and its items stay), and ALT-F10 moves it up or down: every section folds to its head while it moves, ↑ ↓ move it, ENTER leaves it there and ESC puts it back.

ToPress
Next / previous sectionCTRL-PGDN / CTRL-PGUP (or ⌥↓ / ⌥↑)
Go to a section by nameALT-F5
Fold a section to its head / open it (twice: all)ALT-− / ALT-=
Hide or show empty sectionsALT-V
Move the section within the viewALT-F10 on its head, or View ▸ Section ▸ Move

Section Properties (F6 on a section head, or View ▸ Section ▸ Properties) gathers one section's settings in a box: its Section head (type another category's name, or F3; F6 there opens that category's own properties), Item sorting, Filter, how many items it shows, and its Columns, listed in order with the item text as <Items>. In that list DEL takes a column out of this section only, ALT-↑ ALT-↓ move one, and INS adds one. In a section with numbers, Select numeric functions sets the count, total and the rest for all its numeric columns at once (chapter 11).

Two settings make a view more compact. View ▸ Section ▸ Lines draws each section head as a line across the screen in place of the blank line before it, so more fits. View ▸ Hide ▸ Column heads shows the column heads only on the first section on the screen, when every section repeats the same ones.

Columns

ALT-R adds a column to the right of the highlight and ALT-L to the left. A column headed by a category shows, for each item, which of its children the item is in: the Priority column shows High, the Friends column the names. A column headed by a category without children shows * for its items; a date column shows the date. ← → or TAB move between the item and its columns.

Each section has its own columns. A column added to all sections, the usual way, is linked: one column shown in every section, so widening it in Garden widens it in Notices too. A column added to the current section only changes on its own. View ▸ Column ▸ Add asks for all of it at once:

{{49-column-add|box}}
  • Column head: type a category's name (a new name makes a new category), or F3 to pick one.
  • Category type: a new or standard head can be made a date or numeric category as the column is added.
  • Position: right or left of the current column. Insert in: all sections or the current one. Which of these ALT-R and ALT-L use is set in File ▸ Properties ▸ Insert new columns in.

The rest of View ▸ Column:

  • Width: ← → narrow or widen the highlighted column, shown at once; a typed number sets it; ? fits it to its head and the longest entry shown just then. ENTER keeps the width, ESC puts it back. On the item text it sets the width of the item text itself.
  • Move, or ALT-F10 on a column head: ← → move the column within its section, ENTER leaves it there. On the item text, it moves the item text among the columns, so a column can stand before it.
  • Remove (or DEL on a column head): for a linked column Agendum asks whether to remove it from All sections in view or the Current section only.
  • Properties (or F6 on a column head): one box for every column, with its head, width, and Link with other sections; for a date or numeric column, how it shows them too.
{{50-column-props|box}}

An empty section has no items to put the highlight on, so press → on its head to reach its columns.

What a column shows

The Format in Column Properties decides how a standard column shows an item's categories:

  • Name only, the usual: the names (Kanga, Roo).
  • Parent:Category: each name with its parent's before it.
  • Ancestor: only the child of the head they come under.
  • Star or Yes/No: *, or Y and N, for whether the item is under the head at all. SPACE stars and unstars in a Star column; Y and N answer in a Yes/No column.
  • Category note: one line of the note of the item's category there, with the column's own title. Typing in it changes that line of the note.

Rabbit. Suppose I begin every friend's note with where they live: Kanga's would say Home: by the sandy pit. Must I still look it up each time?

Owl. Give the Friends and Relations section a Category note column, line one, titled Where, and add it to that section only. It shows by the sandy pit beside every item about Kanga. The label before the colon is left out, you see. And if she moves, type the new place in the column.

Rabbit. Into the column? And her note changes?

Owl. Line one of it. The rest of her note is quite safe.

Typing in a column

Typing in a column assigns the item. Agendum completes the name as you type, among that column's categories only:

{{22-complete|rows:3-5}}
  • ENTER accepts what is shown; F8 / F7 (or ALT-N / ALT-P) show the other matches.
  • A name that doesn't exist yet becomes a new category under the column's head; INS (or CTRL-N) makes exactly what you typed even when it begins an existing name.
  • INS on an entry adds another one (Kanga, Roo); DEL removes one; F2 changes it.
  • F3 in a column lists its categories to choose from.

Rabbit. I typed Lo and it said Low before I had finished.

Owl. It knows the column. In the Friends column, Ka would have been Kanga. And if you type a friend it has never heard of, it simply adds them. Rather like you, at parties.

Many views

F8 opens the view manager, a box listing the views, with * before a datebook: ENTER switches to a view, INS (or CTRL-N) adds one, F2 renames, ALT-↑ / ALT-↓ reorder, DEL discards (items and categories are kept). Typing the start of a view's name jumps to it: with many views, c goes to the next one beginning with C, and more letters narrow it down. From a view, ALT-N / ALT-P go to the next or previous view and ALT-F8 back to the last one.

Hiding things

View ▸ Hide turns on or off, for this view: Done items, Empty sections, Inherited items (those only in a subcategory of the section's category), Pending items (waiting for others, chapter 12) and Column heads after the first section.

Categories have notes as items do, and ♪ at the right of the control panel says that whatever the highlight is on has one: the item, a section head, a column head, or the category a column entry shows. F5 opens that note, so in the Friends column on Kanga, it is Kanga's note that opens, not the item's.

7. Dates and times

In Which the Second Friday of November Is Found and Rabbit's Paws Have a Rest

Rabbit. This is the part I need. Everything I have to do has a when.

Owl. Then write the when in the item, the way you would say it, and Agendum will put it in the When column. You needn't learn a format. Agendum learned yours.

Dates in what you write

The first date in an item becomes its When date. Agendum reads a great many ways of saying it:

KindExamples
Daystoday, tomorrow, yesterday, the day after tomorrow, Friday, this Friday, next Friday, last Friday
DatesOctober 20, 20 Oct, the 20th of October, the first of November, 2026-10-20, 10/20

Some rules to know:

  • A day on its own is the next one (today, if it is today). This puts it in the current week, next in the following week, last in the week before.
  • A date without a year is in the future: in December, “March 3” is next year's.
  • A time without am or pm falls between 8:00 in the morning and 7:59 in the evening, so “at 3” is 15:00; hours over 12 read as a 24-hour clock.
  • Words that are also ordinary words (“sat”, “wed”, “May”) count as dates only after “on”, “by”, “until” or “till”, or with a day number (“May 5”).
  • A date is read once: change other words of the item and the date stays; change or remove the date words and it follows.

Setting the date yourself

ALT-F2 (or Item ▸ When) asks for a When date, or type it in the When column. A date you set yourself wins over the text from then on; DEL in the When column gives the date back to the text. File ▸ Dating stops Agendum reading When dates from text in this file.

The pop-up calendar

In a date column, F3 opens a month with the entry's date highlighted (or today's, when it has none). ← → move a day, ↑ ↓ a week, HOME / END to the month's first and last day, PGUP / PGDN a month and CTRL-← / CTRL-→ (or < / >) a year; typing a date jumps there. F6 adds a time, ENTER puts the date in the entry as if you had typed it, and ESC leaves it alone. ALT-C shows the calendar anywhere, just to look something up.

{{39-popup-calendar|box}}

Rabbit. Which day is the second Friday of November? I always have to count on my paws.

Owl. ALT-C, then PGDN. There it is. Your paws may have a rest.

Again and again

With every, each or evry, a date recurs: “every Monday”, “every other Monday”, “every 2 weeks”, “the 15th of every month”, “every quarter”, “every year”. Mark such an item done with F4, and Agendum adds the next one right after it, with the same text, note and categories and the next date. Months keep their day: an item on the 30th of every month falls on the 28th in February and on the 30th again in March.

Owl. “Weed the carrots every Monday.” Done, and next Monday's weeding is already waiting.

Rabbit. That is either wonderful or very discouraging.

Alarms

ALT-A on an item with a When time sets an alarm, 5 minutes before unless you type another number (0 to 60); the item shows @. When the time comes Agendum beeps, names the item, and shows @ at the top right until you look at Search ▸ Alarms, which lists the day's alarms. ALT-A again cancels an alarm.

8. A datebook

In Which Rabbit Wants to See Thursday, and Looks Without Changing Anything

Rabbit. All very well, Owl, but a list is a list. When I want to know what I am doing on Thursday, I want to see Thursday.

Owl. Then you want an appointment book. Agendum keeps one inside every file; you only have to open it.

A datebook view is a view whose sections are not categories but times: the hours of a day, the days of a week, or the days, weeks or months of a month or a quarter. Every dated item sits in its place. Make one with View ▸ Add (or INS in the view manager): type its name, then choose Datebook instead of Standard. It starts as this week, Monday to Friday:

{{46-datebook-week}}

The control panel says what the datebook is for (“Datebook for: When”), and the view manager marks datebook views with *. Type an item under a day and it gets that day; in a day view, under an hour, it gets the hour too. Rabbit types “Dig the potato bed” under Wednesday, and it is dated Wednesday at once.

Its settings

A new datebook opens its settings, and View ▸ Properties shows them again:

{{47-datebook-props|box}}
  • Period: a day, a week, a month or a quarter.
  • Interval: what each section covers: 15 or 30 minutes or an hour of a day; a day or a week of a month; a day, a week or a month of a quarter. A week has a section per day.
  • Start at and End at: the hours of a day (8:00 to 17:00 unless you say otherwise) or the days of a week (Monday to Friday). A day view also has a section for items with that date but no time.
  • Base date on: which day, week or month to show: “today”, “tomorrow”, “next week”, or a date. It is read again every day, so a datebook based on today always shows today.
  • Date category: whose dates place the items (When).
  • Section: whose items to show, under every section: When shows every dated item; Friends and Relations would show only visits and calls.
  • End category: a date category saying when an item ends; the item then shows in every hour or day it lasts. Events from calendars on the web keep their end in a category called Ends, ready for this.
  • Hide column heads and Section separators: as in any view (chapter 6).

SPACE picks a category, types a value or goes to the next choice; DEL clears the End category. A datebook's sections are its dates, so it cannot have sections added or removed, and moving an item with ALT-F10 means changing its date instead.

Turning the pages

ALT-B (View ▸ Browse) turns the datebook's pages: ← → show the previous or next day, week, month or quarter, and ALT-F5 jumps to a typed date. BROWSE shows at the top right meanwhile. ENTER stays on the page you have turned to (the datebook is then based on that date until you change Base date on); ESC goes back.

Rabbit. And if I only want to peek at next month?

Owl. Turn to it, look, and press ESC. Nothing is changed by looking. Even you can look without changing anything, Rabbit.

Rabbit. I am not sure that is true.

An ordinary view with a date filter (“today” to “end of this week”) can be browsed the same way: ALT-B moves the filter's dates, ← → a day, ↑ ↓ a week, PGUP / PGDN a month and CTRL-PGUP / CTRL-PGDN a year; the highlighted section's own date filter moves if it has one.

One day's schedule

Search ▸ Schedule shows everything on one day without making a view: choose the date (typed, or F3 for the calendar), and if you like a category to narrow it to, and ENTER lists that day's items in the Show View. ALT-B then steps to the next day, and the next.

9. Conditions and actions

In Which Owl Says the Most Beautiful Thing Rabbit Has Ever Heard

Matching words is not the only way to file. A category can also have rules: conditions, which pull items in because of where else they are, and actions, which do something to items as they arrive. Both are set in Category Properties.

Assignment conditions

Assignment conditions opens the category tree. ENTER on a category cycles: assigned (items must be in it), not assigned (items must not be), none. All conditions must hold, except that the children of an exclusive family are alternatives (High or Medium).

  • Urgent: if High and Garden.
  • Just me: if not Friends and Relations.
  • This week: if When is between “beginning of this week” and “end of this week”. Choosing a date or numeric category opens a box for a range (below).

Items join when they meet the conditions and leave when they stop meeting them; the assignment shows as c in the profile. A range with relative dates is read again every day, so This week moves on by itself. Assignments you make yourself are never taken away by a condition.

Assignment actions

Assignment actions run once, when an item joins the category. ENTER on a category cycles: assign it there too, remove it from there, none. A date or numeric category asks what to set: a date such as “tomorrow” (read when the action runs) or a number.

Special actions marks items done, or discards them, as they arrive. A category whose items should be dealt with at once (“Done already”) can tidy itself.

Owl. Make a category Notices to write, with the actions Notices and When = tomorrow. Put anything in it, and it is a Notice for tomorrow, without another keystroke.

Rabbit. Owl, that is the most beautiful thing I have ever heard.

Rules for items you already have

New conditions and actions only touch items as they change. Category ▸ Execute applies them to existing items: the conditions of one category (or all, from MAIN), against all items, the current one, the marked ones, a section or the view, and actions to new assignments only or to old ones as well. ALT-X executes everything on all items, ALT-E just the highlighted section's category.

{{14-execute|box}}

10. Finding, filtering and sorting

In Which lett* Finds Every Lettuce and Everything Is Put in Order

Searching

ALT-F6 opens the search box: what to look for, in the item text, notes or both, which direction, and whether capitals matter. ENTER goes to the first match; then ALT-N / ALT-P go to the next and previous, F7 marks one, ESC stops. Words match whole; lett* matches any ending; (a phrase) must appear as written. Search ▸ File collects every match in the whole file into a Show View.

Filters

View ▸ Filter narrows the whole view, View ▸ Section ▸ Filter one section. ENTER on a category cycles + (items must be in it), - (must not), none. A date or numeric category asks for a range instead:

{{33-datefilter|rows:3-12}}

Relative dates in a range are read again each day: “today” to “end of this week” always shows the rest of this week. Put $ before one to fix it the day you set it ($Monday). The filter shows beside the view's name, and a new item typed into a filtered view gets what the filter needs, so it doesn't vanish. ALT-B moves a date filter to other days, weeks or months (see A datebook).

Sorting

View ▸ Order sorts the items of every section; View ▸ Section ▸ Sort one section. Sort on the item text, on a category (its children in order or alphabetically, its date, or its number), or on a line of a category's note; then by a second key for ties. Each key can go up or down and put items without a value first or last. The same box can sort the sections themselves.

{{10-sort|box}}

11. Numbers

In Which Rabbit Finds Out What His Carrot Seeds Cost

A numeric category holds a number for each item: cost, hours, how many lettuces. Make one with Category Properties ▸ Type ▸ Numeric, add it as a column, and type numbers in it. Totals appear at the bottom of every section.

{{42-numeric|rows:3-8}}
  • Numbers may have a sign, a currency symbol, thousands separators or parentheses for negatives; F2 edits a number, DEL removes it, and the control panel shows # in a numeric column.
  • View ▸ Column ▸ Properties on a numeric column sets the symbol, decimals, thousands separator and negative style, and which results to show: count, total, average, minimum, maximum. Section Properties ▸ Select numeric functions sets them for every numeric column of a section at once. Display % of total adds a narrow % tot column beside it, with each item's share of its section's total.
  • Whether you write 1,234.50 or 1 234,50 is your own choice, in File ▸ Customize (chapter 13).
  • Items sort by their number, and filters and conditions take numeric ranges.

In Which Christopher Robin's Expotitions Come In from the Web

Wherever Agendum asks for a file name (opening, saving under a name, exporting, printing to a file), F3 shows the Select File box instead: the folders and the files that fit, in the folder of what you had typed. ↑ ↓ move, typing narrows the list to names containing what you type, ENTER opens a folder or puts the file in the prompt, ../ goes up, F7 shows when each file was last changed, and ESC goes back to the prompt as it was.

Quitting, opening another file, starting a new one and importing all replace what is on the screen, so when the file has unsaved changes Agendum first asks “Save changes first?”: Y saves (asking for a name if the file has none) and goes on, N goes on without saving, ESC changes its mind.

A file keeps everything about the information in it: items, categories, views and the file's date and matching settings. File ▸ Transfer ▸ Export writes all of it as readable JSON, good for backups and for other programs; Import opens such a file as a new, untitled file to save under a name.

Calendars from the web

Rabbit. Christopher Robin keeps his Expotitions on a calendar. On the web, he says. I never know when the next one is, and then everyone has gone.

Owl. Then let Agendum read it for you.

A calendar published on the web, with an address ending in .ics or starting with webcal://, can be brought in as items. File ▸ Transfer ▸ Calendars lists the file's subscriptions. INS asks for the address, then for a name, which becomes the category the events go in, and reads the calendar at once:

{{45-calendars|box}}
  • Every event from today up to 90 days ahead becomes an item: its title the item text (filed by text matching like anything Rabbit types), its description the note, its start the When date and time.
  • When an event ends goes in a date category called Ends, made the first time an event has an end: the time a meeting finishes, or the last day of an event lasting several days. A datebook can use it to show the event in every hour it lasts.
  • An event set in a time zone of its own is moved to your time, by the zone's own rules as the calendar gives them, so a meeting at nine in New York shows at the hour it is for you.
  • A repeating event becomes one repeating item, on its next date: “Poohsticks at the bridge” every Wednesday moves on to the next Wednesday when Rabbit marks it done.
  • Agendum reads the calendar again every hour while it runs. A new reading changes only what the calendar changed; Rabbit's own notes, categories and done marks stay, an event that is cancelled or removed takes its item with it, and an item Rabbit threw away does not come back.
  • In the box, ENTER reads the highlighted calendar now, F changes how often it is read (every hour, every 6 hours, every day, only on request), A how far ahead (30 to 365 days), and DEL unsubscribes, asking whether to throw its items away too.

Each reading is one step for CTRL-Z, and a scheduled one that changes nothing passes without a word.

A ready-made planner

Agenda came with ready-made files to begin from, and Agendum comes with one: the Activities Planner, starters/planner.agendum. Copy it and open the copy; it has no items, only the setting-up done for you:

  • Categories for People, Projects, Tasks, Activity (Calls, Meetings, Mail, Reading, Follow up, Issues, Ideas), Priority and Expenses with their types, with short names and Also match words, so “Phone Owl” is filed under Calls and “Hotel” under Lodging.
  • Catch-alls that pick up what the others miss: Unassigned takes tasks that name nobody, No Priority Assigned tasks without a priority.
  • Views: Tasks (where it opens), Activities, Priorities, Calls (with each person's phone number from their note), Completed This Week, Overdue, three expense reports, People, Projects, and datebooks for today, tomorrow, this week, next week and this month.
  • Three macros: change the date settings, hide or show done items, and print the view.
cp starters/planner.agendum ~/planner.agendum

Rabbit. I shall tell Kanga. She has Roo, and Roo is a great deal to keep track of.

Owl. Tell her to begin with the planner. It is easier to change a Method than to invent one.

Your settings, in every file

Some settings belong to you, not to a file: they follow you from file to file and someone else opening your file keeps their own. File ▸ Customize sets them, and saves them in your preferences file (~/.config/agendum/preferences):

{{15-customize|box}}
  • Theme: Classic, the grey, blue and cyan of Agenda 2.0 (with exactly its colours in a terminal that shows 24-bit colour); Mono, for plain terminals, with only bold, dim, underline and reverse; Borland, yellow on blue like the old DOS programming tools; or the colours of a well-liked terminal scheme: Modus Operandi (light), Modus Vivendi (dark), Alabaster, Zenburn or Ayu.

  • Auto-save interval: a file with a name is saved by itself once no key has been pressed for that many minutes (Off, or 1 to 60).
  • Decimal separator and Thousands separator: how numbers are shown and typed everywhere, 1,234.50 or 1 234,50.

14. Macros

In Which Rabbit Teaches Agendum to Water the Carrots, and Tigger Goes Round in Circles

Rabbit. Every morning I go down to the Garden section and type “Water the carrots”. Every single morning, Owl.

Owl. Then teach Agendum to do it. Show it once; it remembers the keys and plays them back whenever you like. That is a macro.

ALT-F3 opens the macro manager. INS (or CTRL-N) names a new macro, and F7 starts Learn mode: the manager closes, LEARN shows at the top right, and every key Rabbit presses does its usual work and is recorded too. ALT-F3 stops recording. Here is Rabbit's Water, and behind the box the item it typed while he taught it:

{{48-macros}}
  • ENTER in the manager runs the highlighted macro; ALT-Z, anywhere, runs the last one again.
  • F2 shows the keystrokes as text, written as Agenda wrote them: text as itself, other keys in braces, such as {DOWN}Water the carrots{ENTER} or {F10}vc. You may change them there.
  • F6 opens Macro Properties: rename it, attach it to a key of its own ({ShfF4}, {CtlE} …), and choose where it may run (views, notes, the category manager, or with a key, everywhere else). A macro on one of F1…F10 shows its name on the key bar.
  • F7 on a macro that already has keystrokes asks whether to replace them or add to them. DEL deletes a macro, ALT-F5 sorts the list, ALT-↑ / ALT-↓ move one.
  • A whole run is one step for CTRL-Z, however much it did.

Rabbit. And if a macro runs another macro, which runs the first one again?

Owl. Then Agendum stops it after a few rounds and says so. A Method should not go round in circles. That is what Tigger is for.

15. A few more things

In Which Rabbit's Head Is Full, and There Is a Little More, All of It Small

Rabbit. Is there more? My head is full.

Owl. A little more, and all of it small. You may forget these until the day you need one, and then you will remember there was something.

In brief

  • Go to a section: ALT-F5 lists the view's sections; ENTER jumps there.
  • Collapse: ALT-− folds the current section (or, in the category manager and lists of categories, a family) to its head, ALT-= opens it; pressed twice, all of them.
  • Category ▸ Recalc files every item again, by today's categories and words, and dates items that have a date in their text but no When date.
  • Category ▸ Add adds a category under MAIN without opening the manager.
  • Discarding a view keeps its items and categories; only the way of looking goes.
  • The Show View is where searches and dependency lists appear; ALT-F8 goes back to the view you came from.
  • Search and replace in a note: ALT-F6 in the note, with Replace text Yes; replace all at once or confirm each one.
  • The key bar: ALT-K hides it for a little more room, and Agendum remembers that next time.
  • Undo goes back up to 100 steps, in views and in the managers; undoing back to the last save clears the *.
  • For the curious: ESC then ? opens a hidden screen of technical details, which also shows each key exactly as your terminal sends it. ESC leaves it.
