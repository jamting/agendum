## § 6 Summary and verdict

Agendum is now a public project, and it went public in good order: a licence and a CLA, a history rewritten on a copy and checked by a dry run before anything was pushed, a site published from `main`, and a version anyone can install with Homebrew. The last review's ten resolutions were almost all carried out, and the largest of them paid off where it mattered: the owner's file shrank from 5.3 MB to 1.4 MB and a save from half a second to under a tenth. The biggest strength is that the project keeps finding its own faults cheaply: a local review of 5,000 lines found ten real findings in six minutes, and the recovery file brought back the owner's work after the one crash in four days of logs. The biggest risk is the shape of that crash: a cursor moved onto a field that does not exist, in a box the fuzz walks reach only by chance, and the same shape (an index looked up, then counted with) appears in a dozen places the reviews have not yet read. The one thing to do next is to let the walks start inside every box, datebook views included, and to finish the review of the keys and the boxes (groups e to g) before new features are built on them.

## § 8 Architecture

**José** checked that the last review's long-carried item is finally closed. `Update` is now a dispatcher of 563 lines, and the shell's answers live in `Update.Results` (625 lines). He then read `Update.Results` and found what the roadmap already admits (ROADMAP, Fixes and polish, "The extras' answers handled beside them"): about half of it is the extras' logic (a feed's and a calendar's read, OPML, publishing), and `Update.Prompts` holds the extras' prompts. These are listed as doors in `test/agendum/architecture_test.exs`, so they are allowed, but they are the one place where the extras' modularity (the owner, 2026-10-27) is not yet true.

He noted that ADR 0044's named exceptions grew from four to five today: automatic completion now asks for the ingested categories so that the person's own come first (`test/agendum/architecture_test.exs`, `@named_exceptions`; R32, R75a). Each exception has its reason written beside it, which is the rule working as meant. He asked only that the list be read at each review, so that it does not quietly become a second architecture.

**Joe** looked at the shell. Saving is quick again (§ 4), so the question of saving in a background task, raised last time, can rest: a save of 84 ms in a loop that reads no key meanwhile is the simplest design that works. He found one slow answer worth keeping an eye on: on 2026-10-26 publishing the owner's meeting blog took 6.5 s in the update step, with 1,418 garbage collections (session log 2026-10-26 21:35). That was before the parsers' fixes of 2026-10-27, so it may already be gone; it should be measured once on the owner's devlog rather than assumed.

## § 9 Code quality

**José** found the code clean by every tool: Credo reports no issues in 6,940 modules and functions, Dialyzer no errors, and the three suppressions left each give their reason (`lib/agendum/store.ex:513`, `lib/agendum/tui/terminal.ex:46`, `lib/agendum/tui/boxes/template.ex:128`).

The tools do not see everything, and the review of group d showed what they miss (`docs/CODE_REVIEW_SLICES.md`, Record):

- **Arithmetic on what may not be there.** "every 0 days" divided by zero in `Recurrence`; more than 15 decimals made `Float.round/2` refuse; a word the regex matched but the language pack did not list made `Map.fetch!/2` raise.
- **Work that grows with the input.** Working days were counted one day at a time, so a large number froze the program.
- **Bytes counted as characters.** A width check used `byte_size/1`, so £ and ¥ columns turned scientific one character early.

Today's crash in View Properties has the first shape: `Enum.find_index/2` returned `nil` and the next line subtracted one from it (`lib/agendum/tui/boxes/view_props.ex`, `step/3`). He counted 59 uses of `find_index` in the TUI, 12 of them followed by arithmetic within two lines. Not all are wrong, since many look up something that is always there, but each assumes it, and none says so. He suggested that the review of groups e to h look at these first.

## § 10 Tests and verification

**Joe** listed what was added since the last review, all from its resolutions or the code reviews: property tests for every parser of outside input, 46 hostile files with time budgets, fuzz walks in the golden file with the Extras on, walks that deliver background results out of order, and `App`'s background answers tested (`ROADMAP_DONE.md`, Sharpen the saw). The suite grew by 227 tests and 6 properties and still runs in about 21 seconds.

He then asked why the walks did not find today's crash. Many start in a datebook view (four of the six choices, `test/agendum/tui/fuzz_test.exs:482`), but they reach View Properties only by pressing F10, v and p in that order among about eighty keys, and then must reach Filter and press ↑ twice. A walk of random keys finds the boxes it falls into. The Extras' boxes are already entered on purpose (`fuzz_test.exs:266`, `press_shell` with F10 F T); the same could be done for every box in the registry, on every kind of view. That would have found this crash, and probably its cousins (§ 9).

**José** added that the nightly fuzz has not yet run in the public repository, where the workflow is new. It should be checked tomorrow morning; a scheduled workflow that never ran looks the same as one that passed.

**Mitch** noted, with approval, that the owner's crash report came with the log, the keys and the screen's state, and that the fix came with a test that presses the same keys. That is the debug log doing exactly what it was built for.

## § 14 Agent readiness

**Claude** said: going public helped my lens more than any feature would have.

1. **The project is now readable by any model.** CLAUDE.md, `docs/INDEX.md` and the ADRs are public, and the site publishes an `llms.txt` made from the index (`Mix.Tasks.Agendum.Pages`, 39 documents). A model that has never seen Agendum can find its way from one file.
2. **An agent did real work on the code this week, in the open.** Today's local review of group d was run by an agent in the background, read 5,000 lines in six minutes, and its findings were checked and fixed in the same session. That is agent readiness of the project's methods, if not yet of the program.
3. **The program still has no headless door.** `agendum` without a terminal can print its version and nothing more (`lib/agendum/cli.ex:23`). 154 commands run through `Core.Command.run/3`, and none can be called from outside. A read-only `agendum --json view NAME FILE` is still the smallest useful step, and it is still waiting on the owner's choice (§ 4, item 9). Now that strangers can install the program, it is also the step that would let someone else's agent use it.
4. **Golden tasks** remain unstarted. I would start them at the same time as the headless command, as one item.

**José** agreed that the headless reader should be in the escript, not a mix task, and that it should share the code of `mix agendum.thoughts`. **Joe** asked that it be read-only for a long while.

## § 16 Methods

**Joe** said the gate held again: 27 versions, every one through `mix check`, the long fuzz run before every tag, and the pre-push hook running the suite before today's pushes.

**José** praised the way the repository went public: a script, a dry run whose report the owner read section by section, five fixes, then a fresh run, then the move (`docs/plans/GITHUB.md` § 7). ## § 17 Points of disagreement

3. **How public the panel's work should be.** **Carsten** valued that the minutes are now public, as the reasoning behind the program is part of its value to its users. **Claude** noted that public minutes are also read by models, which take a lens's opinion as a fact more easily than a person does; the opening paragraph that says the voices are lenses matters more now. ## § 18 Resolutions

Keep it within the fast-test budget; the long runs go to `mix agendum.fuzz`. _S–M · Joe, José_
2. **Finish the local review, one group a session**: e (the keys) now running, then f and g (the boxes), then h, split in two if it stays at 10,700 lines. Each group's findings fixed as a release with its test file, as for d. _M · José, Carsten_
